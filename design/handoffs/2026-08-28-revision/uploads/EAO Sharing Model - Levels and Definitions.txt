EAO Sharing Model — Levels and Definitions

The model has four sharing levels, one sealed compartment, and one credential lane. The levels form a ladder in which each step widens the audience; the compartment sits outside the ladder; the credential lane runs alongside it.

Level 1 — Team Only. The default level applied to every file on admission to the Digital File Library. Access is limited to the group that originated the file — the project team working the assessment, or the business unit that created the record, as applicable. Level 1 is where unapproved, in-progress, and working operational records live. Nothing arrives at any other level by accident: broadening access is always a deliberate act by a permitted user, and it is always logged.

Level 2 — All EAO. Access for every EAO staff member, regardless of project or business unit. Level 2 is the normal home for approved internal records — material that is settled enough to be relied on across the organization, but that has not been released outside the EAO. Elevating a file to Level 2 is a statement that the file is fit for internal reliance.

Level 3 — All IDIR. Access for any BC Government employee holding an IDIR account. Level 3 is used where a record is intended for broader government sharing — where partner ministries, agencies, and crown corporations have a legitimate working interest but the record is not yet, or not ever, for public release. Level 3 is a wide audience and should be chosen because government-wide sharing is intended, not merely because the file is uncontroversial.

Level 4 — Public. Access for anyone, with no credential of any kind, through EPIC.public. Level 4 is where the EAO's finalized products belong: the presumption is that a finalized record is published unless there is a documented reason under policy or the environmental assessment process to withhold it. Because the audience is the world and the act is effectively irreversible, moving a file to Level 4 requires an explicit human action by a permitted user, carries a confirmation warning, and is logged. Publication is driven by the EA process gates; the sharing model enables that decision rather than making it.

Level 0 — Sensitive. Level 0 is not a step below Level 1. It is a sealed compartment that sits outside the sharing ladder, used for the small body of material the EAO must be able to hold in confidence — Compliance and Enforcement investigations being the primary case. Content held at Level 0 is restricted to a named set of roles and is protected by encryption, so that it is unreadable to front-end users including Superusers, and unreadable to back-end users through the data layer. Because Level 0 is a compartment rather than a level, a file cannot be elevated out of it through the ordinary sharing interface. Content leaves Level 0 only through a deliberate records or legal action taken by the accountable authority, at which point it enters the sharing ladder in the normal way at Level 1. This is the one place in the model where the usual principle — that a permitted user may widen access — does not apply.

Special: Selected Credentials. The credential lane exists because the EAO must sometimes work with a party who has no place on the ladder. First Nations, proponents and their consultants, certificate holders, partner agencies, and local governments all need to see specific material during an assessment, and none of them can be served by simply moving a file to a broader level. A Selected Credential grants a named external party sight of specified content at Levels 1, 2, or 3 without making that party a member of the audience those levels describe: the file's sharing level does not change, and no one else's access changes with it. Credentials are time-bound by design, with a defined start and end, and they can be reviewed, renewed, or revoked — singly or in bulk by metadata, project, or assessment — using the same batch controls that govern other access changes. Level 4 requires no credential, since the content is already open to anyone. Level 0 is not reachable by credential.
